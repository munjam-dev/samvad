> **For authorised agencies only.** These guidelines explain how we respond to lawful requests for Samvad user information. Users can read them to understand how their data is protected.

## 1. Our approach

We respect the rule of law and the privacy of our users. We disclose user information only in response to valid legal process under Indian law, or in a genuine emergency involving a risk of death or serious physical injury. We review every request for legal sufficiency and narrow or reject requests that are overly broad, unclear or not legally valid.

## 2. How to submit a request

Send requests from an official government email address to [{{LEGAL_EMAIL}}](mailto:{{LEGAL_EMAIL}}), with:

- the name, designation, agency, official address and contact details of the requesting officer;
- the legal provision the request is made under (for example, section 94 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, section 69 or 79 of the Information Technology Act, 2000, or rule 3(1)(j) of the IT Rules, 2021);
- the **phone number** (with country code) of the account concerned;
- the specific information requested and the relevant date range;
- a signed order, notice or authorisation from the competent authority.

We do not accept requests through in-app chats, or from personal email addresses.

## 3. What information may exist

What we hold depends on how the account was used and how long ago. Generally:

| Category | What may be available |
|---|---|
| Basic account information | Phone number, account creation date, profile name, about text, profile photo, registered devices (app and Android version), and last connection time |
| Group information | Groups the account belongs to, group names, members and admins |
| Message content | Message content is stored on our servers until deleted. Messages in groups and in older chats are not end-to-end encrypted, so that content may be produced where Indian law requires it, under a valid order. In a one-to-one chat that is end-to-end encrypted we hold only encrypted data we cannot read, so we can produce only information about it (who, when and approximate size) |
| Call records | Who called whom, when, for how long and the outcome, kept as call history, including after an account is deleted. We do not record or store call audio or video |
| Personal Vault | Stored only on the user's phone; we do not hold it and cannot produce it |
| Content no longer held | Deleted messages and data past our retention periods (see the [Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy-policy)) generally cannot be produced |
| Location | We do not collect location |

## 4. Preservation requests

We will preserve available records related to an official investigation for **90 days**, pending formal legal process, on receipt of a preservation request containing the details in section 2. Preservation can be extended once for another 90 days on request.

## 5. Emergency requests

If there is an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury, an authorised officer may email [{{LEGAL_EMAIL}}](mailto:{{LEGAL_EMAIL}}) with the subject **"EMERGENCY"**, describing the emergency, the account involved and the information needed to prevent the harm. We review emergency requests as a priority and may disclose limited information without formal legal process where the law allows.

## 6. Child safety

We report child sexual abuse material to the appropriate authorities as the law requires, and respond to related requests as a priority. See our [Child Safety Standards](/legal/child-safety-standards).

## 7. Content removal orders

We act on orders from a court of competent jurisdiction, and on notices from the appropriate Government or its authorised agency, to remove or disable access to unlawful information, within the timelines required by rule 3(1)(d) of the IT Rules (within 36 hours, or sooner where the law requires).

## 8. Notifying users

Unless we are legally prohibited, or notice would create a risk of harm, we may tell users when we receive a request for their information, so they can seek legal advice.

## 9. Requests from outside India

Requests from authorities outside India must be made through the applicable mutual legal assistance treaty (MLAT) or letters rogatory process with the Government of India.

## 10. Transparency

We intend to publish a periodic transparency report with the number of government requests we receive and how we responded, and the monthly compliance reports required of intermediaries where applicable.
